Many experienced entrepreneurs want to move to Canada by buying or building a business. The options changed a lot in 2025 and 2026: some programs paused or closed, while others stayed open. Here is an overview of the landscape **as of October 2026**.

> Programs open, pause and close with little notice. Treat this article as a starting point, and confirm every detail with a licensed immigration professional and the official program website before making decisions.

## Federal: Start-up Visa is paused

The federal **Start-up Visa Program** stopped accepting new applications after December 31, 2025. Applicants with a valid 2025 commitment certificate had until June 30, 2026 to apply. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) has said it is designing a new, more targeted pilot for entrepreneurs, but no details have been published yet.

## Provincial entrepreneur streams

Most entrepreneur pathways to permanent residence now run through **Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)**. Each province sets its own rules, and they differ widely:

- **British Columbia.** The BC PNP **Entrepreneur Immigration** program runs a **Base** category and a **Regional** stream for smaller communities. Both held invitation rounds through 2026. Published thresholds include a personal net worth of $600,000 and an investment of $200,000 for Base, with lower thresholds for Regional.
- **Alberta.** The **Rural Entrepreneur** and **Graduate Entrepreneur** streams are active in 2026, but entrepreneur nominations are very limited. The Rural stream requires an endorsement from a participating rural community.
- **Ontario.** Ontario closed its **Entrepreneur Stream**, along with the rest of its previous streams, on May 30, 2026 as part of a program redesign. A new entrepreneur stream focused on buying existing businesses has been signalled for a later phase, but its criteria have not been published.
- **Saskatchewan.** The Entrepreneur stream closed permanently in March 2025.
- **Manitoba.** The Business Investor Stream has been operating through an interim, case-by-case process rather than regular draws.

Several other provinces, including some in Atlantic Canada, run their own business streams with different investment, residency and job-creation requirements.

## Work permits while you set up

Some buyers first come to Canada on a temporary work permit tied to the business. The old "owner-operator LMIA" stream no longer exists as a separate route. Today, entrepreneurs who own a controlling interest usually look at the **C11 "significant benefit" work permit** under the International Mobility Program. It requires showing that the business will bring real economic benefit to Canada, and it is assessed case by case.

## The Rural Community Immigration Pilot is for employees, not owners

The **Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP)** launched in 2025 with 14 participating communities. It is a pathway for *workers* with a job offer from a designated local employer, not for business buyers. It can still matter to you as an owner: if you buy a business in a participating community, the pilot may help you recruit staff.

## Questions to answer before you buy

1. Which province and community do I want to live in, and is there an open stream there?
2. Does the business I'm considering meet the stream's investment, ownership and job-creation rules?
3. Does the stream require me to register or be invited *before* I purchase?
4. What is my plan if the stream pauses while I'm in the process?
