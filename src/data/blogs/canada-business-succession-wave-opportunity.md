Across Canada, a generation of business owners is getting ready to step back. For people who want to buy a business, especially entrepreneurs moving to Canada, that creates one of the largest ownership transitions in the country's history.

## The numbers

In October 2026, the Canadian Federation of Independent Business (CFIB) published new research on small business succession. The headline findings:

- **63%** of small business owners plan to exit their business within the next ten years.
- Only **6%** have a formal succession plan in place.
- More than **$2 trillion** in business assets is at stake.
- **Retirement** is by far the main reason (78%), followed by burnout or stress (21%) and high operating costs (20%).

When CFIB asked owners about their biggest obstacles, **47%** said finding a buyer or a suitable successor. 37% said they were too busy to plan, and another 37% said they didn't know how to value their business. More than half (54%) expect to sell to an unrelated buyer.

Put simply: there are many good businesses, many owners who want to sell, and not enough prepared buyers.

## What this means for buyers

**More choice, especially outside big cities.** Many retiring owners run profitable, unglamorous businesses in smaller communities: daycares, motels, senior care agencies, trades, food manufacturing, convenience retail. These businesses rarely appear on national listing sites and often change hands through brokers, accountants and local networks.

**Sellers care about more than price.** CFIB found that 87% of owners want to protect their employees and 79% want to support their community. A buyer who shows a genuine plan to keep the team and serve the town can win a deal, or better terms, against a higher bidder.

**Vendor financing is more common.** Owners with no successor are often willing to finance part of the price themselves (a "vendor take-back"), which lowers the cash you need at closing.

**Time pressure works both ways.** Without a plan, some owners will close rather than sell. CFIB found that 13% expect to shut down entirely, rising to 17% among retailers. Buyers who can move with clear financing and advisors in place have an edge.

## Where the opportunities are

Demand-driven sectors stand out:

- **Child care**, where expanded public funding has increased demand for licensed spaces.
- **Senior and home care**, driven by Canada's aging population. Almost one in five Canadians was 65 or older in 2025.
- **Hospitality and tourism**, with tourism spending forecast to grow again in 2026.
- **Wellness and personal services**, with loyal local customer bases.

We look at these sectors in more detail in our [sector spotlight](/blogs/sector-spotlight-daycare-senior-care-wellness-hotels).

## How to be the buyer sellers choose

1. Get your financing pre-qualified before you make offers.
2. Show a transition plan that keeps staff and serves existing customers.
3. Be ready to offer the owner a role during the handover.
4. Work with advisors who can move quickly through due diligence.
